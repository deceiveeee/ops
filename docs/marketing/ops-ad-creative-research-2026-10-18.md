# OPS advertising research and original ad briefs

Research date: **18 October 2026, America/Phoenix**. Audience: **students learning finance**, explicitly selected by the user. Product inspected: primary checkout `main`, commit `be4609d06476e4eddde98728859ac1fb143e265b`. This is a creative research deliverable, with three original scripts and capture plans. It is not a report of an OPS campaign or a production-site verification.

## The direction

Make the product demonstration carry the promise: **a student changes something in OPS, sees the financial result, and gets an explanation they can use**. Each ad should show one existing capability and lead to its corresponding experience.

The strongest first series is:

1. **See why a bond price changes.** Actual yield buttons, fixed payments, changed model price and explanation.
2. **Keep the evidence behind your analysis.** Actual company-report reader, selected passage and saved investigation.
3. **Test what a valuation depends on.** Actual assumption choices, reinvestment, cash and estimated value.

These demonstrate interactive learning, source-based research and assumption testing. They give the student a reason to visit the website that is visible in the ad itself. The first is the best starting hypothesis because the action and result are simple enough to follow in a short clip. That ranking is our judgment, not a measured OPS acquisition result.

Core campaign line, written for OPS: **“See the idea. Test it. Explain it.”** Supporting product description: **“Interactive finance lessons and company research.”** These are proposed creative copy, not claims of demonstrated learning improvement.

## How the evidence was evaluated

Research prioritized reports by the brand, producing agency, advertising platform or research authors. Searches covered website/software advertising and adjacent learning products; educational examples were prioritized after the audience clarification. Website lead generation, app subscriptions and software downloads are different outcomes, so their acquisition costs cannot be transferred to OPS.

Three evidence levels are distinguished throughout:

- **Controlled comparison:** an explicit split test or experimental study. It supports the tested bundle, population and outcome, with the published limitations.
- **Reported campaign outcome:** conversions or cost reported by the advertiser/agency/platform, with insufficient public detail to isolate the creative's effect.
- **Creative reference:** an inspectable film, transcript or storyboard. It can explain a communication technique even when conversion results are unavailable.

Views, click-through, leads, subscriptions, completed lessons and measured learning are different outcomes. An ad remaining active in an ad library does not disclose its profit, spend or incremental results. Public success stories are selected by companies with commercial interests. Most cases below do not disclose budgets, sample sizes, attribution windows or uncertainty.

The academic reason to keep those distinctions is substantive: Gordon, Moakler and Zettelmeyer's 2023 analysis compares observational estimates with **663 Facebook experiments** and finds that the observational approaches generally fail to recover experimental effects. This supports an experimental measurement strategy; it does not estimate OPS performance. [Kellogg research record](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2023/close-enough-a-large-scale-exploration-of-non-experimental/)

## What successful campaigns actually establish

### Babbel: a small sample of the learning experience

Babbel's agency describes a Facebook/Instagram campaign with a lead-in video, two simple Spanish tasks using app imagery, and a trial/download invitation. It reports **11% lower cost per purchase**; the client also compares engagement with normal prospecting ads. Exact purchase comparator, campaign dates, sample, spend and randomization are undisclosed. This is reported conversion evidence, not proof that interactivity alone caused the improvement. [Shuttlerock case](https://www.shuttlerock.com/copy-of-babbel)

Inspection: read the complete case description and sampled its embedded preview, including the branded trial end card. The linked standalone playable failed to resolve, so it was not independently completed.

**OPS interpretation:** let viewers see one attainable learning action and its payoff. Our initial format would be a recorded demonstration; a Reel is not itself a playable ad. The next website experience must deliver the task shown.

### Culture et Formation: one course, made for the placement

The e-learning provider's agency reports **56% lower cost per lead and 21% higher click-through** versus original assets. It converted existing imagery into short Facebook Feed/Instagram Stories videos with early brand recognition, brief benefits and a direct CTA. Publication/campaign dates, spend, sample and test design are absent; several creative properties changed together. Its separate “lead quality” figure is undefined and is excluded from our decision. [Shuttlerock case](https://www.shuttlerock.com/culture-et-formation)

**OPS interpretation:** build a mobile-readable demonstration of one lesson task. Format, framing and destination belong in the creative brief. This case does not establish an optimal OPS duration or a guaranteed reduction in acquisition cost.

### Babbel: a human service explanation

For a German creator-style campaign featuring actor Felix von Jascheroff, memmo.me reports **100% higher return on ad spend and 61% lower cost per acquisition**, compared with similar ads/the rest of the account. Dates, attribution, audience, offer and experimental controls are undisclosed; scaling also changed sales volume. [memmo.me case](https://business.memmo.me/success-stories/babbel-100-roas-increase)

**OPS interpretation:** a real person explaining a concrete task is a useful variant to test alongside product footage. Celebrity casting and informal delivery are not established necessities.

### Skillshare: the offer and audience also matter

The campaign consultant reports **over 7,200 subscribers in seven months**, including over 1,400 attributed to one ad in two months. Much of the work focused on website/churned-member retargeting and a **three-month membership for $0.99**. Its CPA comparisons use an undisclosed internal target, not an experimental control. Campaign year, spend, retention and incrementality are unpublished. [First-party campaign report](https://sarahbrodyconsulting.com/skillshare-case-study)

**OPS interpretation:** an impressive ad result may depend on a warm audience and an offer. An OPS cold-student campaign cannot inherit those economics. State OPS's actual access conditions and measure the full journey.

### Canva: match a specific need all the way through

Apple reports Canva had **30% higher install-to-trial rates and 8% higher retention than similar channels**. The case describes matching search intent to particular design needs and using funnel data to improve the onward experience. Publication/campaign dates, retention window, comparable cohorts and study design are undisclosed. [Apple Ads case](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/success-stories/canva)

**OPS interpretation:** a bond-price ad should reach the bond lesson; a report-reading ad should reach reports. App Store search has different intent from Instagram discovery, so these figures do not forecast OPS results.

### Grammarly: measure more than attention

Microsoft's **4 September 2025** case reports **73% conversion lift**; separately, **CPA was 23% lower year over year**, alongside search and site-visit gains. It describes recurring lift studies, professional-audience segmentation, budget changes and weekly optimization. It does not publish study design, conversion definition, samples or campaign dates. This is evidence about a measured media program, not an isolated video hook. [Microsoft Advertising case](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/resources/discover/case-studies/grammarly-success-story)

**OPS interpretation:** distinguish watching the ad, visiting the lesson and doing meaningful work. Professional audience results are not a student-acquisition benchmark.

## What the actual creative teaches us

### Grammarly Sportscasters: entertainment leads into product proof

The producing agency says its brief required product demonstrations to occupy most of each spot. It reports the Sportscaster set outperformed evergreen creative on click-through, viewthrough, install efficiency and awareness, without effect sizes or test details. Its statement that longer versions performed well is a useful warning against a universal duration rule. [Squero case](https://www.squero.com/work/grammarly-growth)

Inspection: sampled frames from the agency-hosted Basketball execution, including opening, commentators, writing UI, the visible **Simplify** action, and a closing URL/app-compatibility card. This was not a complete audio/transcript review. [Exact hosted film](https://player.vimeo.com/video/1053142653?h=9a8233d2b6)

**OPS interpretation:** an interesting opening must lead to visible product work. In our clips, the control, changed output and explanation should receive the most screen time. The agency's set-level performance report cannot be assigned to this particular film or scene.

### Slack: features acquire meaning inside a recognizable task

The research reviewed the complete 2:20 transcript of Slack's official “So Yeah, We Tried Slack …” video and its opening still. It moves from a team's communication problem into channels, file sharing, integrations and switching devices, then relates those actions to daily work. The producing agency describes audience anecdotes, not controlled registration results. [Official film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B6zVzWU95Sw), [Sandwich account](https://sandwich.co/clients/slack/)

**OPS interpretation:** connect each feature with something the student is trying to understand or produce. A list of tools alone leaves the benefit implicit. Its extended setup is not a template for a short Reel; our product action can start in the opening shot.

### Grammarly's student campaign: audience relevance needs actual research

The full official June 2023 case-film transcript describes the back-to-school “Future You Thanks You” campaign: student situations, humorous future selves, alternating entertainment/product moments, close framing and a direct next step. It reports over **150 million video views**, with no acquisition-cost or conversion result. [Google/brand/agency transcript](https://business.google.com/en-all/think/ai-excellence/grammarly-case-study/)

**OPS interpretation:** use students' real questions and language. Humorous characters are optional; finance comprehension is the proposed payoff. The view count demonstrates distribution, not improved grades, learning or subscriptions.

## Platform studies: useful boundaries, not performance promises

**Reels format.** Meta's official indexed guidance reports **34.5% lower cost per result** for 9:16 video with audio and safe placement of key content versus images, across **15 Reels-only split tests** in e-commerce, retail and consumer goods. This tests a bundle, not sound or aspect ratio separately, and does not study student finance education. The direct page sometimes redirects to login; the primary indexed page includes the methodology footnote. Use vertical composition, intelligible audio, captions and current placement previews; do not put the percentage into an OPS forecast. [Meta Reels guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads)

**Video structure.** Google's ABCDs guidance covers attention, early/integrated branding, audience connection and clear direction. For action-oriented ads it emphasizes tangible product demonstration. Its cited Google/Kantar April 2021 research reports improvements in sales likelihood/brand contribution, which are different measures from actual sales. We use the structure as a brief-writing aid and make no numerical OPS prediction. YouTube guidance is not an Instagram-specific controlled result. [Google ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)

**Attention research.** A Vidmob/TikTok report analyzes **1,678 ads and 7.3 billion impressions** in 2023, with awareness objectives and 2-/6-second viewthrough KPIs. Product shots are associated with higher six-second viewing; it is observational and does not establish lesson conversion. The report gives inconsistent October 15/20 end dates. We use it only to motivate an opening-product-shot test, not a sales claim. [Original three-page report](https://vidmob.com/hubfs/Vidmob_May2024/Pdf/Vidmob-x-Tik-Tok-Hook-Analysis-.pdf?hsLang=en)

**Audio experiment boundary.** TikTok's Canva case describes comparing audio while holding message/format constant, and reports favorable conversion/cost direction. Its prominent doubling of active users and 80% cost reduction are week-on-week campaign results, not published randomized treatment effects for music. We should test audio after the product story works. [TikTok Canva case](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/inspiration/canva-commercial-music-library?redirected=1)

These sources do not establish that 15 seconds always wins, expensive production is required, a celebrity improves results, or a good organic post will be the best acquisition ad.

## The creative rules we will apply to OPS

The following are original design decisions inferred from the evidence and the product audit. They remain testable hypotheses.

| Decision | Reason for this audience | Concrete execution |
|---|---|---|
| Open with a recognizable finance question and visible OPS UI. | A student can identify both the subject and what the website does. | A bond's fixed payments and model price appear before the first click. |
| Center one action and its consequence. | The viewer can follow the relationship without remembering a tour of the entire site. | Yield choice → price change → the lesson's explanation. |
| Explain necessary terms in place. | A novice should not need an untaught word to understand the invitation. | Briefly gloss market yield as the return investors require. |
| Show enough of the authentic interface to establish the feature. | The website earns the promise through observable behavior. | Readable control, result and saved-state feedback from an actual session. |
| Relate the result to understanding or a piece of student work. | The user-selected audience is learning finance. | Explain a price change; retain evidence for an analysis; compare valuation assumptions. |
| Keep the same promise after the click. | The destination can continue the activity the ad established. | Exact lesson/report route, with any introductory stage acknowledged. |
| Build a family of concepts, then vary one element at a time. | We need to learn whether the topic, hook or execution is working. | Three feature concepts; two opening lines for the first finished ad. |

No generic profit imagery is required to demonstrate these capabilities. Motion can follow the actual price change, highlight the source passage or connect a changed input to its output. Creative treatment should make the task easier to follow.

## Original ad 01 — “Why did the price fall?”

**Job:** attract a student to an interactive lesson through a finance relationship they can see. **Proposed master:** 22 seconds, vertical 9:16. Timing is an editorial starting point. **Destination:** `/lessons/if-2-2-why-market-rates-change-bond-prices`. **Success action:** complete the introductory comparison and the Reprice task.

| Time | Actual footage and edit | On-screen copy / voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Start on the real Reprice interface at 4%, with price and promised payments legible. Integrate a readable Open Portfolio Studio wordmark into the composition. | Text: **“Same payments. Why can the price fall?”** Voice: “This example bond pays forty dollars a year.” |
| 3–7s | Brief highlight around the market-yield label and actual 4%/5% controls. Keep the payment context in view. | Text: **“Market yield = the return investors require.”** Voice: “Change the return investors require.” |
| 7–12s | Actual click from 4% to 5%. Show price $1,000.00 → $922.78 and bar change. | Text: **“4% → 5%”** and **“$1,000 → $922.78.”** Keep **“Teaching example”** readable. |
| 12–18s | Hold the price, unchanged payment labels and real explanation. A subtle highlight links them. | Voice: “The same future payments now have a lower value today. OPS lets you test the relationship.” |
| 18–22s | End card retains a crop of the lesson alongside the full product name and matching next step. | **“Open Portfolio Studio”** / **“Explore the interactive bond lesson.”** |

**Full narration:** “This example bond pays forty dollars a year. Change the return investors require. The same future payments now have a lower value today. OPS lets you test the relationship. Explore the interactive bond lesson.”

**Caption:** “Change the market yield. Watch the bond price respond. Explore the relationship in an interactive OPS lesson. Demonstration uses a hypothetical bond.”

**Two opening variants:** A: “Same payments. Why can the price fall?” B: “Why does this bond's price change?” Keep the demonstration, voice, offer and destination the same when testing those openings.

**Why this incorporates the research:** a small, comprehensible sample of the learning experience; prominent product evidence; mobile framing; one next step. Those choices follow the Babbel precedent and platform guidance, while the exact script is original OPS work.

**Capture truth:** the landing page starts at the introductory Value stage. Complete it naturally before recording Reprice. The ad may feature a later lesson moment, but must not claim that the first click opens the Reprice control. No completion-time promise.

**Independent number check:** for ten annual $40 payments plus $1,000 principal at year 10, `P(y) = Σ[t=1…10] 40/(1+y)^t + 1000/(1+y)^10`. This gives $1,000.00 at 4% and $922.7826507 at 5%, matching the inspected UI when rounded. This is a model check, not a market forecast.

## Original ad 03 — “What changes the value?”

**Job:** show that students can examine assumptions and their consequences. **Proposed master:** 25–30 seconds, vertical 9:16. **Destination:** `/lessons/if-5-1-estimate-a-valuation-range`. **Success action:** work through the introductory stages and inspect all three Growth cases.

| Time | Actual footage and edit | On-screen copy / voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Open on the actual Growth stage with growth 4% and required return 10% visible. | **“Same growth. Different values. What changed?”** |
| 4–10s | Highlight the real 8/10/12% return-on-new-capital choices. | Brief gloss: **“What new business investment earns.”** Voice: “Growing a business takes reinvestment.” |
| 10–19s | Click from 8% to 12%; show reinvestment 50% → 33.3%, cash left $60m → $80m and model value $1,000m → about $1,333m. Keep **“OPS teaching example”** visible throughout. | Voice: “Change what that investment earns. It needs less reinvestment for the same growth, leaving more cash. Watch the estimated value respond.” |
| 19–24s | Hold the real explanation and fixed assumptions. | Persistent label: **“OPS teaching example.”** Voice: “Explore the assumptions in OPS.” |
| 24–29s | End card with the model in view. | **“Open Portfolio Studio”** / **“Try the valuation lesson.”** |

**Full narration:** “Same growth. Different values. What changed? Growing a business takes reinvestment. Change what that investment earns. It needs less reinvestment for the same growth, leaving more cash. Watch the estimated value respond. Explore the assumptions in OPS. Try the valuation lesson.”

**Caption:** “Explore how growth, reinvestment and cash flow connect. Change the assumptions in an OPS valuation example and examine the result.”

**Capture truth:** reach Growth through Frame, Claim and Baseline; do not present it as the first screen. Preserve the fixed assumptions and example label. Values describe an invented teaching business, not a real company's predicted price.

**Independent number check:** the specified lesson convention gives reinvestment fraction `4% / return on capital`, cash `$120m × (1−fraction)` and value `cash / (10%−4%)`. At 8/10/12%, cash is $60m/$72m/$80m and model value is $1,000m/$1,200m/$1,333.33m. It checks this lesson's convention, not real-world forecast accuracy.

## Production specification and honest destinations

Start with genuine captures from the deployed experience. Crop or enlarge the action/result for mobile while retaining the inputs and qualifications needed to understand it. Use existing Inter/Fraunces typography and the current OPS visual language. Any overlay should annotate what the product actually does; do not invent interface controls or output.

Use a 9:16 master, clear narration, readable captions and audio that does not compete with the explanation. Validate important text, UI and the end card against the current Instagram placement preview, including interface overlays. Do not assume that shrinking a desktop browser capture makes it readable on a phone. Ad duration, pace and voice style remain test variables.

For an organic Reel, make the profile-link journey explicit and test it as a viewer. For a paid placement, configure its available CTA and website destination to match the on-screen invitation. An ad for a later stage can lead to the relevant lesson's beginning; the copy must accurately describe that journey. None of the scripts assumes a new stage-deep-link feature.

The user calls the product OPS/Open Portfolio Studio, while current metadata/navigation also use **Investing Studio**. Before production, settle a consistent visible account/end-card/site naming treatment. This research does not rename the application.

“Free to explore. No account needed” is present in current homepage source and can be an access message after the exact deployed guest journey is verified. Do not promise universal free access or a particular time to completion from that sentence alone.

## How we will learn which ad works

**Primary outcome:** a new visitor completes the meaningful activity advertised. For ad 01 that means the introductory comparison and Reprice task, not just landing on the page or clicking any button. Completion measures product use; it does not by itself prove learning. Evidence of improved understanding needs a separate learner study.

Proposed measurement chain:

`ad exposure → destination visit → relevant task start → meaningful task completion → later learning activity`

Use watch/hold rates and outbound clicks to diagnose where an ad loses people. Compare spend per meaningful completion and the proportion of visitors who complete the task when choosing what to continue. Retention is useful later, provided returning learners can actually be measured. No universal target CPA or expected conversion rate can be estimated from the public cases.

**First comparison:** produce ad 01 with its two opening variants. Keep audience, placement, budget, destination, measurement window and body constant in a platform experiment where available. Predefine the decision metric, collection window and detectable difference based on an actual budget/baseline before launching. Do not split a small sample among many variants or stop as soon as an early result looks favorable.

Then compare the winning execution direction with the other feature concepts as a broader campaign decision. That comparison changes topic and destination as well as creative, so it does not isolate a hook. A later creator/voice test can compare a real human explanation with product/narration alone; an audio or length test comes after that. These are proposed tests, not scheduled or launched campaigns.

An A/B comparison identifies the stronger tested variant under its conditions. It does not automatically identify how many students would have arrived without advertising. A suitable holdout/lift design answers that separate incrementality question. Meta documents the distinction among A/B tests, creative tests and lift studies. [Meta Blueprint](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/219763-ab-test-conversion-brand-lift-course)

**Current instrumentation limit:** the inspected layout mounts Vercel Analytics. A bounded source search did not find activity-completion marketing events; page-view analytics alone do not establish the proposed funnel. Confirm production instrumentation and attribution before paying to judge acquisition. No pixel, event, cookie or privacy implementation was added by this research, and student answers/financial plans should not become advertising payloads.

## Release facts that affect capture

Source review found two specific alternative scenes unsuitable for advertising until repaired: a diversification example displays four −9% holdings but a −8.5% portfolio, and a duration simulator reverses the usual positive-convexity approximation explanation. The [audit](</C:/Open Portfolio Studio/tmp/ops-ad-creative-research-2026-10-18/feature-audit.md>) records exact files and lines. The proposed flagship uses the independently checked exact-price scene, not either affected example. No lesson code was changed.

Production readiness still requires a fresh logged-out traversal of each actual destination, working SEC access for the report ad, final mobile reading/crop checks, and confirmation of the shown saved-state behavior. These are concrete capture dependencies, not assertions that the entire product has passed QA. The separate portfolio reconstruction, advanced optimizers and Treasury paths are outside the released-feature evidence for this brief.
