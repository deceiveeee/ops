# Mission 13 — Write the rules and defend the portfolio: lesson plan (Gate B)

**Source authority:** `docs/source-audits/mission-13-capstone.md` (Gate A closed 2026-08-17).
**Phase prompt:** `docs/agent-prompts/portfolio-builder/08-mission-13-capstone.md`.
**Mission record:** `pb-13`, artifact `policy`, "Operating Plan and IPS", 40 target minutes.

## What the learner walks out able to do

Operate a portfolio they already built — decide what to do when something happens to it, write
the rules down before they need them, compile twelve missions into one document they could hand
to someone else, and then do the same job on a stranger's portfolio without being told which
skill applies.

## Upstream inputs — all twelve, and the readiness wall

Both completion states require **every** Mission 1–12 checkpoint valid and current. That is the
mission's hardest design problem, not its source work: a learner arriving with gaps meets a wall
twelve items wide.

**Design decision: the wall is the first screen, and it is a map rather than a refusal.** Each
missing or stale checkpoint is named, linked, and explained in one line — *what it is, why this
mission needs it*. A learner with gaps can still enter in practice mode and see exactly what
`Practice-complete` will cost them. Nothing is silently disabled.

| Reads from | Supplies |
| --- | --- |
| M1 mandate and readiness | Goal, horizon, cash needs, loss capacity |
| M2 beliefs and falsifier | The market view the whole plan rests on |
| M3 bond policy · M4 required return | Return and risk requirements |
| M5 allocation | Strategic weights, ranges, loss budget |
| M6 evidence · M7 valuation gate | Research-only and rejected ideas |
| M8 friction budget | Cost of every action the plan permits |
| M9 evidence test | The bar a thesis-break claim must clear |
| M10 architecture licence | Passive core, benchmark, edge conditions, review date |
| M11 timing policy | Whether deviation is permitted at all |
| M12 holdings slate | Exact identity, costs, source dates, overlap |

## Prerequisite terms — defined at point of use

The same constraint as Mission 12, and the same resolution. Eight terms, each defined where the
learner first needs it, against their own saved work rather than in a preamble.

| Term | Defined as | First used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating policy | The rules that say what you do when something happens, written before it happens | Beat 1 |
| Drift | The gap between the weights you chose and the weights you now hold | Beat 3 |
| Rebalancing | Bringing the portfolio back to the mix you chose (IG-1) | Beat 3 |
| Benchmark | The thing your result is compared against, named in advance | Beat 2 |
| Thesis break | The stated fact that would mean the reason you bought is gone | Beat 5 |
| Replacement | Swapping a product while keeping the exposure | Beat 5 |
| Governance | Who decides, who reviews, and on what schedule — here, you | Beat 4 |
| IPS | The document that records all of it so a stranger could follow it | Beat 4 |

## Beats

Sequence follows the phase prompt's learning sequence exactly: define → model → guided practice
→ write and compile → independent scenarios → transfer and defence.

### Beat 1 — What an operating policy is *(introduce)*

Opens on the readiness map above, then the distinction that carries the mission: **every earlier
mission decided something; this one decides what happens next.** A plan without operating rules
is not a plan, it is a snapshot.

Claims: S1-2 (a philosophy is what you return to when strategies stop working), S1-3 (without
one you are easy prey), S38-1 (a philosophy that does not match its owner will fail).

### Beat 2 — One scenario, worked all the way through *(model)*

A single fully worked example — market crash — showing all six answers the flight test wants:
what changed, which policy controls, the financial consequence, the action or non-action, the
downstream work affected, and the evidence that would change the response.

Then the IPS rule that scenario implies, written out. The learner sees the shape once before
producing it.

Claims: S38-3 sleep test, S38-4 life change test, **SEC-4 a stop order becomes a market order**,
SEC-1 and SEC-2 on unguaranteed prices.

**SEC-4 is the beat's teaching moment.** The instrument a frightened investor reaches for turns
into the order type with the least price protection, at the moment prices move fastest. It also
forces the distinction against Mission 11: a stop *rule* is a condition you check; a stop *order*
is a standing instruction to a broker.

### Beat 3 — The Rebalancing Control Room *(guided practice)*

Operates the learner's own saved weights and slate. Three methods compared side by side:

1. sell overweight and buy underweight;
2. add new money to underweights;
3. redirect contributions or distributions.

Each shows drift repaired, remaining deviation, estimated friction from the Mission 8 budget,
tax flags, liquidity effect, and which written rule the action satisfies.

Claims: IG-1 through IG-8, S36-8, S6-3, S6-5.

**Never declares a method universally optimal** — the phase prompt forbids it and the sources do
not support it. Method 3 is shown first because it avoids selling entirely, but "first" is not
"best": it is unavailable to a learner who is no longer contributing, and the interface says so.

**The band or cadence is the learner's**, per IG-7's "a certain percentage that you've identified
in advance". Labelled personal policy on the page. Vanguard is not cited here at all — see D3.

### Beat 4 — Write the rules, then compile *(Workbench application)*

Two genuinely new IPS elements, and only two — Gate A established that the other fourteen already
exist in saved artifacts:

- **CFA 2b, the review and update process.** The element with real force for a solo investor: it
  is what stops a policy being rewritten mid-shock to justify a decision already made.
- **CFA 4c, the rebalancing process.** Written from Beat 3.

Plus contribution, withdrawal, sell/replace and thesis-break rules.

Then the compiler runs. **It compiles; it does not re-ask.** Any question already answered in
Missions 1–12 is read, never posed again. A missing input names its mission and links there.

Every material assumption is marked **source**, **learner** or **OPS**. Every high-decay fact
carries an as-of date — which is where Mission 12's holdings dates surface as live warnings
rather than trivia.

Claims: C-1 through C-5, S38-14, S38-15, S6-6.

### Beat 5 — Eight more scenarios, no hints *(independent perturbation)*

The remaining eight run without labels or prompts. Coverage is recorded in the Gate A matrix;
the mission adds nothing not sourced there.

Scenario 8, the Edge Licence expiry, uses the **25% no-continuity null** from Mission 9's
evidence test as the mechanism. Session 36's percentages appear as dated illustration or not at
all, under D1.

### Beat 6 — A stranger's portfolio, and a defence *(assessment)*

A genuinely new investor and portfolio, with **no labels saying which mission skill applies**.
Diagnose, repair, operate.

**Critical failures are a gate, not a deduction.** Any one of these fails the case regardless of
everything else, because each represents a learner who would do real harm:

weights not totalling 100% · near-term liquidity in incompatible risky assets · hidden or
unacknowledged leverage or margin · wrong security, share class or product identity ·
unsupported concentration or active edge · stale provenance presented as current · no defined
response to loss, drift or broken thesis · treating the exercise as advice or trade authorization.

The third and fourth are Mission 12's lessons arriving as assessment: a plan that cannot name its
own share class has not identified anything.

**The 80/100 threshold is an OPS pilot design choice and is labelled as such on the page**, not
only in the evidence file. It is not represented as validated, because no learner calibration has
been run.

## Two risks recorded now rather than discovered at Gate E

**1. Forty minutes is tight for this content.** Nine scenarios, a three-method control room, a
twelve-input compiler and a transfer case. The compiler is what makes it fit — it must genuinely
read saved work rather than re-ask, or the mission doubles in length. If something has to give
at Gate D, the honest lever is the transfer case moving to an optional lab, not thinning the
flight test.

**2. Plan the stage split now, not after measuring.** Mission 12 was designed as six stages and
measured at 1.9–2.0 screens on two of them, forcing a split to eight after the fact. This mission
is larger. It should be built as **eleven stages** from the start — readiness map, operating
policy, worked scenario, three control-room methods, rule writing, compilation, two flight-test
groups, transfer case, defence — with each stage held to one screen of content at 1440×900 and
measured before Gate E rather than after.
