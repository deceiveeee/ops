# Mission 11 — Set a market-timing policy: lesson plan (Gate B)

**Source authority:** `docs/source-audits/mission-11-timing.md` (Gate A closed).
**Phase prompt:** `docs/agent-prompts/portfolio-builder/06-mission-11-timing.md`.
**Artifact produced:** Timing Policy — either *no timing* or a fully bounded tactical rule.

## What the learner walks out able to do

State, in writing, what would have to be true for them to deviate from their Mission 5
strategic weights — how far, for how long, on what evidence, and what ends it — or state that
they will not deviate and why that is a decision rather than an absence of one.

## Upstream inputs and downstream effects

| Reads from | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| Mission 5 strategic weights | The baseline any deviation is measured against |
| Mission 8 friction budget | The real cost of moving out and back |
| Mission 9 evidence test | Whether a proposed signal clears the learner's own bar |
| Mission 10 Architecture License | Must be valid and current; timing operates on the architecture, never around it |

Changing allocation, architecture, evidence or friction marks the timing policy
`Review required` and blocks Mission 12. Changing timing marks holdings, order rehearsal,
flight test and operating plan `Review required`.

## Prerequisite terms — each defined positively before use

| Term | Defined as | First used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic allocation | The weights your horizon, cash needs and risk tolerance imply, set in Mission 5 | Beat 1 |
| Tactical deviation | A deliberate, bounded move away from those weights | Beat 1 |
| Signal | A written, checkable condition that must be true before you deviate | Beat 3 |
| False positive | The signal fires and the expected move does not happen | Beat 3 |
| Opportunity cost | The return given up while out of policy | Beat 2 |
| Expiry | The date the deviation ends whether or not you were right | Beat 4 |
| Stop rule | The observation that ends the deviation early | Beat 4 |

## Beats

Sequence: introduce → model → guided practice → Workbench application → independent
perturbation → assessment.

### Beat 1 — Timing is a deviation, not a separate game *(introduce)*

Anchors on the learner's own saved Mission 5 weights. Timing is defined as a tilt away from
weights they already chose for stated reasons.

Claims: S30-1. Deliberately **not** used here: S30-2 (see D1 — the 93.6% figure is the
misread; where a number is needed for why the mix matters, use S30-3's 40/60 through 1998).

### Beat 2 — The cost of being wrong runs in both directions *(model)*

The **Missing-Time Timeline**. Anchored to saved strategic weights, it walks five paths:
staying at policy; moving to cash and missing a strong interval; acting on a false signal and
reversing late; a bounded tilt that expires; and a headline that never meets the written
signal.

Claims: S30-4 (Shilling, 50 weakest months, 1946–1991, 11.2% → 19% — labelled as hindsight
and by period), S30-5 (Sharpe: 7 in 10), S30-6 (Chua/Woodward/To: 70–80% to break even),
S30-7 (transactions costs, wired to the Mission 8 budget), S30-8 (taxes, directional warning
only — never a personal calculation), S30-10 (payoff and cost are both immense), S33-4 (the
cost of acting on an overvaluation call), S34-9 (all-or-nothing raises the cost of being
wrong — the canonical basis for requiring a maximum deviation).

The symmetry is the point: S30-4 shows avoiding the worst months nearly doubles returns,
S30-5/6 show what hit rate that requires. Neither lands alone.

### Beat 3 — Test a signal against its own record *(guided practice)*

The learner takes a plausible macro rule of thumb and checks it against the record, using
Mission 9's evidence test.

Claims: S32-3 (the rules of thumb, presented **only** as claims to be tested), S32-4 and
S32-7 (the two tables that refute them), F11 (Damodaran's own quiz answer: "no basis"),
S32-2 with its R² of .0728 and t-statistics of 1.29/1.81, S32-5 paired with S32-6 (a signal
that worked 1950–1975 and stopped), S34-5 (newsletters: 58% before upturns, 53% before
downturns).

**F12 is the conceptual spine of this beat.** Prices embed expectations, so the *level* of
growth is not the signal — the *surprise* is. Teach the mechanism, then let the tables land;
otherwise the learner memorises one table instead of understanding why macro rules of thumb
fail generally.

**F10 hazard — must be handled here.** Quiz 32 Q2 (mean reversion taken at face value) and
Q3 (no empirical basis) answer different questions. The lesson must name the difference:
internal consistency of a belief versus evidence for it. A learner who meets both without
that distinction will conclude the course contradicts itself.

Presented with F4's limitation intact: an 82/83-year US sample, period not stated on the
source slide. Small cells (7 and 9 years) and standard deviations to 29.84% shown, so the
learner reads "not reliable" rather than "negative growth is bullish."

### Beat 4 — Write the policy *(Workbench application)*

Two equally legitimate outcomes.

**No timing.** Requires a stated reason, and the learner must acknowledge that "wait until it
feels safe" is not this — it is an unbounded deviation with no expiry.

**Bounded tilt.** Requires all of: signal, benchmark, maximum deviation, eligible sleeve,
expiry, falsifier/stop, review date, friction impact drawn from Mission 8.

No speculative sleeve is offered at any size. Basis: F8 — it appears only in Session 34
narration, never on a slide, and is conditional on a hit rate Session 30 shows is very hard
to reach.

### Beat 5 — An unfamiliar headline *(independent perturbation)*

A novel headline and an ambiguous signal that does **not** meet the learner's written
conditions. The correct action is to take none, and to say which written condition failed.

Modelled on quiz 34 Q3 and Q4, whose answer shapes are already validated: bad timing advice
persists (S34-5), and real timing calls are short-term and far too frequent to act on
(S34-6). Supporting context: S34-7, sixteen strategists on the same date recommending 50–80%
stocks and 0–25% cash.

### Beat 6 — Assessment

Passing requires a policy-consistent action **and** an explanation of what would cause a
review. Clicking through does not pass. Assessed ideas and where each was introduced,
modelled, practised and applied are listed below.
