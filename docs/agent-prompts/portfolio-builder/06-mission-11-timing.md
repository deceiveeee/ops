Paste after `00-master-operating-prompt.md`.

## Objective

Close Mission 11's narration and learner-sequence gates, then let the learner write one of
two defensible policies:

- no market timing; or
- a tightly bounded, testable tactical tilt with a signal, maximum deviation, expiry, and
  stop rule.

The learner must see the cost of being wrong and the cost of missing strong periods. Timing
is a deviation from the Mission 5 strategic allocation, not a standalone return game.
It also follows the Mission 10 architecture: even an explicit no-timing policy must operate
on a valid, current architecture rather than bypass it.

## Source boundaries

- Historical timing relationships are period- and regime-sensitive.
- Do not present past yield-curve, valuation, interest-rate, or macro relationships as a
  live signal.
- Session 30's allocation statistic is frequently misread; use the audit's corrected
  interpretation.
- Reject Session 34's 5–10% speculative sleeve as a beginner default.
- Do not teach that waiting for comfort is a neutral action; make opportunity cost visible.
- A bounded tilt is an OPS learner policy, not a proven forecast.

## Learner experience

Build a **Missing-Time Timeline** anchored to the saved strategic weights.

Compare at least:

- staying at policy weights;
- moving to cash and missing a strong interval;
- acting on a false signal and reversing late;
- a bounded tilt that expires;
- a headline that does not meet the written signal.

Use illustrative, source-labeled scenarios rather than live forecasts. Show:

- policy weight versus tactical weight;
- maximum deviation;
- friction from the saved Mission 8 budget;
- time out of policy;
- expiry and automatic return rule;
- evidence/falsifier status from Mission 9;
- impact on the learner's goal/loss policy.

The control must change a visible path and consequence. Do not use a slider that simply
changes a return number.

## Learning sequence

1. Define strategic allocation, tactical deviation, signal, false positive, opportunity
   cost, expiry, and stop rule.
2. Model why a confident call can still be a poor policy when timing and friction matter.
3. Guided practice chooses whether a supplied signal meets written evidence conditions.
4. Learner saves no-timing or a fully bounded timing policy.
5. Independent perturbation presents a novel market headline and ambiguous signal.
6. Assessment requires policy-consistent action and an explanation of what would cause a
   review.

## Completion and invalidation

Completion requires:

- a valid, current Mission 10 Architecture License;
- reference to current strategic weights;
- no-timing or fully specified bounded rule;
- for a bounded rule: signal, benchmark, maximum deviation, eligible sleeve, expiry,
  falsifier/stop, review date, and friction impact;
- no open-ended `wait until it feels safe` state;
- unfamiliar headline assessment pass.

Allocation, architecture, evidence, or friction changes mark the timing policy invalid or
`Review required` and block later product verification until it is reconfirmed. Timing
changes then mark holdings/order/flight-test/operating work `Review required`.

- imply historical relations are stable;
- make a tactical sleeve required;
- make speculative allocation a beginner default;
- use unreconciled local ASR as citable evidence;
- implement product selection;
- commit or push.

Run the complete master verification. If narration remains unresolved, stop with an audit
and no partial UI.
