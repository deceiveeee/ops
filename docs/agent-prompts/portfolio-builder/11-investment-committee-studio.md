# Optional prompt — competition-neutral Investment Committee Studio

Paste after `00-master-operating-prompt.md`. Run only after the individual Portfolio Builder
core is complete and only with explicit authorization for this optional extension.

---

## Objective

Design and build a neutral **Investment Committee Studio** that lets teams apply the
Portfolio Builder process to a fictional client, document their own research and decisions,
measure portfolio outcomes, write a concise report, and defend the work orally.

This is not Wharton Competition preparation, coaching, submission generation, or a promise
of competitive success.

## Current eligibility boundary

Before every implementation cycle, re-check the current official rules for any competition
the user names. As of 2026-08-12, Wharton's 2026–27 Rules & Roles prohibit participants from
enrolling in a non-Wharton extracurricular course claiming to teach the Investment
Competition, and suspected use can disqualify a team. Current trading rules and learning
materials were also incomplete.

Official references:

- [Wharton Rules & Roles](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/rules-roles/)
- [Wharton learning resources](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/learning-resources/)
- [Wharton registration page](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/register-now/)

Therefore:

- do not use Wharton branding, case data, simulator rules, deadlines, approved universe,
  report limits, pitch duration, or rubric unless the current official cycle is complete
  and the user has written organizer permission for the intended use;
- do not market OPS as competition prep or as meeting professional requirements;
- do not create strategy, research, report, or presentation content on behalf of an active
  team;
- active participants should seek written clarification from the organizer before using an
  explicitly competition-oriented external course;
- use fictional cases and simulator-agnostic workflows by default.

If the requested mode would create a credible eligibility risk, stop and report the exact
rule rather than building around it.

## Studio outcome

A team produces its own:

- dated rule/authority ledger;
- team charter and decision rights;
- fictional client mandate;
- client-to-strategy traceability matrix;
- benchmark and risk policy;
- research memos and rejection log;
- industry/competitor analysis;
- valuation and downside cases;
- trade proposal and decision journal;
- holdings/exposure/attribution review;
- concise final investment memo;
- timed internal defense and Q&A record;
- citation, media-rights, and AI-use ledger.

OPS provides structure, simulations, feedback, and rubrics. Students provide the investment
ideas, research, decisions, writing, and defense.

## Gate 0 — Authority and Rule Lock

Build a user-entered, dated rule ledger:

- official URL and retrieved date;
- cycle/version;
- client/case version;
- eligible instruments;
- simulator mechanics;
- deadlines;
- deliverable constraints;
- advisor/outside-help limits;
- AI, citation, media, and authorship rules;
- school/team eligibility and documentation;
- unresolved organizer questions.

The system does not hardcode last season's rules. Any missing, conflicting, or stale rule
blocks a competition-specific export. A neutral fictional lab remains available.

## Lab 1 — Investment Committee OS

Create:

- team charter;
- rotating roles and research ownership;
- meeting agenda/minutes;
- proposal → questions → dissent → vote → decision flow;
- evidence and AI-use log;
- workload/contribution history;
- advisor view that cannot make student decisions.

Simulation: a 12-minute internal committee where every member must question or defend a
decision. Do not score confidence or speaking style as finance truth.

## Lab 2 — Client-to-Strategy Blueprint

Build:

- one-page fictional client brief;
- case fact → goal/constraint → strategy rule → portfolio expression matrix;
- one-sentence strategy;
- benchmark;
- allocation, sector, position, liquidity, and loss policies;
- scenario/liability map;
- strategy falsifier.

Perturbation: change a cash need, horizon, or client priority and require coherent policy
revision plus downstream invalidation.

## Lab 3 — Research-to-Decision Pipeline

Each representative security memo includes:

- business and industry structure;
- current filing evidence and source date;
- peer/competitor comparison;
- valuation range;
- catalyst/correction mechanism;
- downside and disconfirming evidence;
- client and strategy fit;
- friction/capacity considerations;
- rejected alternative;
- thesis-break condition.

Every proposal passes mandate, eligibility, evidence, valuation, risk, friction, and exact-
identity gates. Keep a rejection log and pre-mortem.

## Lab 4 — Portfolio Review and Defense

Provide:

- weights/exposure and concentration dashboard with tabular alternative;
- benchmark-relative outcome;
- security, sleeve/sector, cash, and friction contribution;
- risk, drawdown, turnover, and strategy-drift review;
- decision journal preserving evidence available at the time;
- explicit separation of short-period luck from long-term thesis evidence;
- concise memo compiler that cites team-authored artifacts;
- timed internal pitch only when the team selects a duration;
- hostile/ambiguous Q&A, `remove your best chart`, and random-member defense drills.

OPS may ask questions and flag unsupported claims. It must not write the team's final answer
or generate pass-off-as-student work.

## Ethics and submission gate

Pass/fail checks:

- exact rule/version match;
- eligible instruments;
- fictional versus official case clearly separated;
- student authorship attestation;
- source citations and media rights;
- AI use disclosed according to current rules;
- advisor/outside-help boundary;
- no fabricated teamwork, research, trades, or results;
- no client contact where prohibited;
- accessibility/readability and file metadata;
- every member can defend a random decision.

Critical failure blocks any export labeled ready.

## Internal rubric

If a rubric is useful, label it OPS internal—not an organizer rubric:

- client fit and traceability: 20;
- strategy coherence and differentiation: 15;
- company/industry analysis and valuation: 20;
- portfolio construction, risk, and implementation: 15;
- evidence, process, and decision learning: 10;
- teamwork and governance: 10;
- communication and Q&A: 10;
- compliance/ethics: pass/fail.

Do not call a score `semifinal-ready`, `winner-ready`, or professionally certified.

## Design direction

Use an investment-committee room, case-file trail, evidence wall, decision ledger, and
portfolio-defense room as metaphors. Preserve Apple-level focus, Khan-style practice and
feedback, and OPS source discipline. Avoid team gamification, leaderboards, fake trading
floors, or a generic project-management dashboard.

## Explicit non-goals

Do not:

- market or name this as Wharton prep;
- encode incomplete/current competition rules without canonical verification;
- use organizer logos, client cases, proprietary material, or simulator credentials;
- make decisions, research, reports, slides, or answers for a team;
- guarantee advancement or outperformance;
- call the course a professional qualification;
- transmit trades;
- add real-time market APIs unless separately authorized and source/licensing reviewed;
- commit or push.

End with an eligibility/ethics gate status separate from the feature's technical status.
