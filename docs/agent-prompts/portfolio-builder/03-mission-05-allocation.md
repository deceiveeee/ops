## Finance boundaries

- No source supplies a universal emergency-reserve amount. The learner chooses and explains
  a target; readiness reflects the gap rather than enforcing an invented number.
- Capacity for loss and willingness to accept volatility are distinct.
- A strategic allocation is a policy range, not a return forecast or optimal truth.
- Do not imply the efficient frontier is stable, uniquely correct, or sufficient for a
  person with liabilities and behavior constraints.
- If optimization is used, disclose objective, inputs, constraints, uncertainty, and why a
  nearby robust allocation may be preferable to a fragile mathematical optimum.
- Expected returns, volatility, correlations, and drawdowns are estimates, not guarantees.
- Do not optimize directly to an unsupported "maximum acceptable drawdown" forecast.
- No regulator supplies a universal personal position-size cap.
- Position loss contribution is transparent:

  `position weight × assumed position loss = contribution to portfolio loss`

  A candidate ceiling may be derived from the learner's loss budget under an explicit
  stress assumption. Label it OPS/learner policy.
- Fund-law 5%/10%/25% diversification tests are not personal suitability rules.

## Learner experience

### Mission 1 dependency — Readiness Runway

Use equal **Build mine** and **Practice case** paths. Ask only for ranges or plain-language
inputs where possible:

- goal and target date;
- expected contributions and planned withdrawals;
- near-term cash needs;
- learner-selected emergency-reserve target and current state;
- high-interest debt status;
- employer-match opportunity where relevant;
- age/jurisdiction, earned-income relevance, and account authority as non-legal flags;
- ability to bear loss;
- willingness to bear loss;
- existing exposure categories, never credentials or exact account identifiers.

Output one route:

- personal deployment available;
- personal constrained with an explicit deployment action plan; or
- practice only.

No route blocks education. A learner can finish all 13 missions through the paper path.

The runway belongs in Mission 1 or in a clearly labeled, non-penalizing prerequisite bridge
that returns its result to the Mission 1 mandate checkpoint. A migrated learner who reaches
Mission 5 without the new readiness fields must complete that bridge before allocation;
their old lesson-completion credit is preserved.

### Mission 5 Preflight — portfolio-theory bridge

Do not assume the learner completed Finance Foundations merely because those routes exist.
Before Allocation Studio, run a labeled, non-penalizing Preflight covering only the needed
mental models:

- portfolio risk depends on weights, asset volatility, and how assets move together;
- diversification changes combined risk but does not eliminate loss;
- an efficient frontier is an estimate-based opportunity set, not a personal answer;
- a risk-free/tangency result depends on inputs and assumptions;
- expected return and required return are uncertain estimates.

If the learner cannot explain these relationships, route them to a short Mission 5 bridge
using already source-gated Finance Foundations material, with links to the full lessons for
deeper review. The bridge must define, model, and give guided practice; it is not a new
mission and does not penalize progress. A learner may instead complete the relevant Finance
Foundations lessons. Allocation remains locked until the learner passes a fresh, unaided
Preflight item after either route.

Do not require the learner to derive covariance matrices, trace the efficient-frontier
optimization, or know convex optimization notation.

### Part B — Allocation Studio

The main visual must show policy roles and loss consequences, not just colorful weights.
As the learner changes a sleeve, reveal:

- target percentage and approximate dollars for a learner-chosen or fictional amount;
- near-term goal funding versus risky capital;
- target range and purpose;
- sleeve contribution to the selected stress loss;
- goal impact in plain language;
- issuer, sector, employer-stock, and overlap categories only where canonically supported or
  explicitly labeled as an OPS diagnostic;
- what downstream decisions will require review.

Dragging may be offered, but direct numeric entry and keyboard buttons must be equivalent.
The learner must be able to explain the loss budget without reading the chart.

### Learning sequence

1. Define capacity, willingness, liquidity bucket, sleeve, strategic weight, target range,
   stress assumption, loss contribution, and concentration.
2. Complete the non-penalizing portfolio-theory Preflight/bridge when needed.
3. Model a complete fictional household/young-investor case, including why a job loss or
   cash need reduces risk capacity without changing preferences.
4. Guided practice repairs one issue at a time: weights not totaling 100%, liquidity
   mismatch, then excessive stress contribution.
5. Learner applies the method to Build mine or Practice case and saves it.
6. Independent perturbation changes an unfamiliar case's cash need or income stability.
7. Assessment requires the learner to repair the allocation and explain the cause-and-
   effect reasoning without hints.
